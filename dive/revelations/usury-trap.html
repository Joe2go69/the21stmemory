The Mathematics of Self-Lending Fraud

The internal mechanics of usurious lending rely on a simple yet total deceptive structure. In a standard transaction where an entity lends £100 at a 25% interest rate, the borrower is required to repay £125. In legitimate commerce, the borrower gains £100 in temporary liquidity in exchange for a £25 service fee. However, within the inverted financial system, the initial £100 is already the borrower's own capital, drawn from their birth certificate bond.

When an individual borrows £100 of their own money under a 25% interest agreement, the true balance sheet reveals zero net capital gain and an immediate net capital loss of £25. The borrower hands over their own £100, receives no external funds, and is forced to surrender an additional £25 of their earned assets to recover what was already theirs. Legally and structurally, this transaction constitutes theft by deception and identity fraud, operating purely because the victim remains unaware of the asset's true origin.
